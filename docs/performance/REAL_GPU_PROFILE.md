## Pass history (older entries condensed — full before/after tables for each are in git history if needed)

- **Visual overhaul** (3D asset pack + 2-light setup + closer camera): FPS held/improved; primitives dropped dramatically (real low-poly character models replacing higher-poly procedural capsules).
- **NPC visual rework** (per-instance material variation + idle/seated animation): no measurable regression.
- **Production-kit integration** (garage/office clutter, break room, safety_lab model, 30 character variants): no meaningful regression, draw calls/primitives rose in proportion to new static dressing.
- **City-backdrop window pass** (SubViewport-baked skyline): zero measurable cost — renders once (`UPDATE_ONCE`), never per-frame.
- **Garage vertical-slice recovery** (3 starter desks + chairs/monitors/whiteboard/lounge dressing): no meaningful regression, ~0.3 FPS avg delta.
- **World+NPC overhaul** (exterior/neighborhood set + 3 looping traffic vehicles): no meaningful regression, still at/near 60 FPS everywhere (see table below).

## Before/after the generated-humanoid StaffAgent integration (real rigged/animated/faced characters wired into `character_model_pool`, [P-REWORK-02])

Before (same three scenarios, same hardware, prior to wiring `assets/models/generated_humanoids/*.glb` into `staff_roles.json`):

| Scenario | Before FPS avg | After FPS avg | Before draw calls | After draw calls | Before primitives | After primitives |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small (0 staff) | 55.5 | 55.7 | 597 | 625 | 12,086 | 12,307 |
| Mid-game (20 staff) | 59.8 | 58.8 | 921 | 1,140 | 17,228 | 29,896 |
| Stress (150 staff) | 58.8 | 48.8 | 3,626 | 5,142 | 52,708 | 206,476 |

Real, measurable regression at high staff counts, and it's expected, not a bug: the generated humanoids are actual skinned/rigged meshes (real body geometry + separate hair/clothing pieces per limb) driven by a live `AnimationPlayer`, not the old flat billboard-style limb-pivot pack — each one costs meaningfully more primitives and at least one more draw call than its flat-mesh equivalent. The 0-staff scenario is unaffected (no characters at all). The 20-staff scenario stays comfortably at 60 FPS. The 150-staff stress case (an extreme that has no realistic in-game headcount analog this early — the real economy caps headcount far below 150 for most of a campaign) drops to a still-playable ~49 FPS avg / 46 FPS min, no stutter spikes beyond what the small-scene warmup frame already shows. Judged acceptable to ship as-is per this session's "profile, don't guess" rule (`docs/performance/REAL_GPU_PROFILE.md`'s own standing policy: object pooling/LOD only after profiling proves need) — noted in `docs/production/KNOWN_ISSUES.md` as a real cost to revisit if a future pass pushes realistic headcount past ~50-80 staff.
