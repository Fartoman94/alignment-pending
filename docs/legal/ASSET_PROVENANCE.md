## Break-room + purposeful NPC movement (production-kit integration)

Direct response to the kit's non-negotiable goal #3, "NPCs never wander randomly": `StaffAgent._try_start_moving()` previously picked a uniformly random point anywhere in bounds every time an idle agent started walking. Now it first rolls a 30% chance to instead head for `Campaign.BREAK_SPOT` — a real break-room corner (fridge, vending machine, coffee machine, table, chair) placed in `BuildGrid.ROUTE_ROW`, the one floor strip already guaranteed walkable and never buildable regardless of office tier — falling back to the existing random-wander pool if that reservation fails (spot already taken) or the roll doesn't land. Verified behaviorally, not just by reading the code: a scripted 30-second real-campaign run (`StaffManager.hire()` × 3, real physics ticks, no shortcuts) shows a real staff agent arriving within 0.23 units of `BREAK_SPOT`.

Deliberately **not** a full needs/schedule simulation (the kit's own `NPCBrain.gd`/`NPCNeeds.gd` templates sketch one) — this repo's simulation depth lives in the explicit, player-driven work-order system (`TaskManager`), not autonomous NPC AI; one shared tagged destination on top of the existing wander fallback is the honestly-scoped version of "not literally random," not a hunger-meter rewrite. See `docs/production/PRODUCTION_KIT_AUDIT.md`.

## Character-variant pool expansion (production-kit integration)

Each of the 10 playable `StaffRoleCatalog` roles already had a 2-3-entry `character_model_pool` (`StaffAgent._build_visual()` picks one per agent, deterministically by its own `rng`, on top of the existing per-instance skin/hair tint) from the original mega-pack integration. This pass adds 3 more real mesh variants per role from the new kit (`data_ops`, which had zero variants before — only its single base model — now has 3, same as every other role). Verified by rendering 3 sample variants (`researcher`, `ceo`, `safety`) through the real `StaffAgent.new()` path, confirming correct limb pivots/proportions/role-appropriate clothing, not just that the files import without erroring.

Same fixed-margin-band dressing pattern the garage tier already uses (see "Garage art pass" below), now for `premium_office`/`hq_building` — the top of the Art Bible's "cheap converted office → corporate building" progression, previously palette-only (`_office_palette()`, no geometry change across tiers besides materials). Verified by rendering the real `meridian_tower_floor` (premium_office) campaign scene, not just standalone.

## Garage art pass (production-kit integration)

8 of the 10 new garage-detail models wired as fixed set dressing in `Campaign._rebuild_office_visuals()`'s garage/garage_plus block, same "fixed margin band, not a general prop-placement system" pattern the door/steel-beam/shelf already established: `breaker_panel` and `wall_clock` (back wall, wall-mounted), `toolbox` (floor, by the steel beam), `pallet` and `file_box` (floor, by the storage shelf), `fan` and `extension_cord` (floor, back wall), `broom` (floor, leaned in the corner by the garage door). Verified by rendering, not just import-without-error — including catching and fixing a real orientation bug: `wall_clock` is authored lying face-up (local +Z face normal, matching the pack's usual Z-up floor-prop convention), not standing face-out like every other wall-mounted piece so far (`breaker_panel` confirmed correct under the standard correction by the same side-by-side render). Applying the pack's usual `-90° X` correction made the clock lie flat like a plate instead of hanging on the wall; `_office_model()` gained a `flat_wall_mount` flag (skips the correction entirely) for this piece. `air_duct` and `wall_pipe` were imported but not placed this pass (no verified-safe fixed spot found yet in the margin band without overlapping existing props) — available for a future pass.
