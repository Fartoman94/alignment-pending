# World, narrative and satire bible

## Setting
Near-future fictional world. Countries and cities may be fictionalized in the initial release to avoid unnecessary real-world political assertions. The office begins in **Port Meridian**, a dense coastal tech city.

## Player company
Player names the company at new game. Default internal placeholder: **Null Meridian Labs**. Do not make this the final public brand without clearance.

## Rival generation
Rivals are assembled from original syllable banks and corporate archetypes. Examples for development only:
- Lattice Nine
- Civitas Compute
- Morrow Metric
- Helioform Systems
- Parallax Works

Each rival receives randomized doctrine: Safety-first, Speed-first, Open-tech, Enterprise, Consumer, Research prestige.

## Narrative acts
### Act I: Garage Intelligence
Tiny office, fragile cash, first useful model.
### Act II: The Benchmark War
Competition, launch pressure, employee politics, PR.
### Act III: Infrastructure Company
Datacenters, energy, national regulation, lawsuits, supply chains.
### Act IV: Agents Everywhere
Autonomous systems transform staffing and risk.
### Act V: Alignment Pending
Capabilities exceed governance. Player choices determine institutional and societal outcome.

## News tone
Deadpan, concise, factual within fiction. No direct mimicry of actual publications.

Example authored templates:
- `{RIVAL} announces a model {DELTA}% cheaper hours before your scheduled launch.`
- `Internal memo titled “Not Technically On Fire” leaks after a cooling incident.`
- `Regulator requests explanation of feature your own board learned about this morning.`

## Character writing
Staff have beliefs and incentives, not alignment labels like “good/bad”. A safety researcher can be ambitious. A growth executive can sincerely value social benefit. Avoid cardboard villains.
