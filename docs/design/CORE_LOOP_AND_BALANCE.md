# Core loop and balance

## Fundamental equation
A model release creates value and risk simultaneously.

`profit potential ≈ demand × capability × availability × price - inference cost - incident cost`

`incident pressure ≈ exposure × hidden risk × autonomy × deployment scale × safety debt`

These are design relationships, not literal final formulas.

## Model attributes (0–100)
- Capability
- Reliability
- Safety confidence
- Latency efficiency
- Cost efficiency
- Autonomy
- Interpretability

Hidden attribute:
- Latent risk (partially revealed by evaluations)

## Safety debt
Skipping evaluations saves time/cash but accumulates Safety Debt. Debt raises odds/severity of incidents and makes future audits slower.

## Hype debt
Overpromising marketing can accelerate users/revenue short-term. If performance misses claims, Hype Debt converts to trust loss.

## Compute economy
Compute is split into training and inference. A huge release can starve research. This creates meaningful scheduling decisions.

## Anti-snowball mechanisms
- Scaling power/land/cooling costs.
- Senior staff scarcity.
- Regulation scales with impact.
- Large models carry inference burden.
- Competitor catch-up bonuses when player dominates.
- Organizational complexity penalty unless management systems improve.

## Difficulty modes
- Founder: forgiving finances, transparent warnings.
- Executive: intended balance.
- Frontier: harsher uncertainty, faster competitors, less precise evaluations.

## Randomness policy
Randomness must be seeded. High-impact random events expose probability or warning signs beforehand where reasonable. Avoid arbitrary campaign destruction.
