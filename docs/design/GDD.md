# Game Design Document

## Working title
**ALIGNMENT PENDING**. Provisional until formal trademark/title clearance.

## Fantasy
The player becomes CEO/technical director of a fictional AI startup. They physically expand an office, hire staff, acquire compute, choose research priorities, train models, evaluate safety, decide release policies, handle incidents, negotiate regulators, survive competitors, and eventually confront systems too capable to treat as ordinary products.

## Genre blend
- 3D office management sim
- Strategy/economy
- Narrative decision game
- Roguelite campaign variation

## Session structure
A campaign is 8–15 hours for first completion, with 20+ hour deep runs. In-game time advances in pausable speed tiers: paused, 1x, 2x, 4x.

## Core loop
1. **Observe** office/company/world state.
2. **Plan** hires, compute, research, safety, financing, facilities.
3. **Train** a model using selected architecture tier, compute budget and dataset categories.
4. **Evaluate** capability, safety, reliability, cost, latency and hidden risk signals.
5. **Decide** internal-only, limited beta, broad release, open release, or delay.
6. **Operate** the released model, pricing, rate limits, customer segments and support burden.
7. **Respond** to incidents, media, employee concerns, competitors and regulators.
8. **Reinvest** revenue/reputation into the next generation.

## Primary resources
- Cash
- Compute capacity
- Power capacity
- Research points
- Safety confidence
- Public trust
- Employee morale
- Regulatory pressure
- Legal exposure
- Market share
- Capability score
- Autonomy score

No single resource is a universal win meter.

## Failure states
- Insolvency with no rescue path.
- Regulatory shutdown.
- Catastrophic trust collapse causing financing/customer death spiral.
- Severe internal incident that triggers a campaign-ending outcome.
- Optional "walk away" endings.

## Victory/end states
Multiple endings, including:
- Responsible Stewardship
- Market Monarch
- Open Release Cascade
- Regulatory Capture
- The Long Pause
- Automation Dividend
- Quiet Acquisition
- Alignment Achieved?
- Simulation Within Simulation (secret)

No ending is labeled objectively "good" or "evil". The epilogue reports measurable consequences.

## Player verbs
Build, place, hire, assign, research, train, evaluate, release, price, restrict, investigate, negotiate, communicate, audit, upgrade, pause, fire, promote, expand, lobby, disclose, recall.

## Physical office layer
Rooms and objects are not decoration only. Desks enable workers, server racks add local compute/heat/noise, meeting rooms improve coordination, safety lab unlocks evaluations, cafeteria affects morale, media room improves crisis response, secure lab reduces leak risk.

## Simulation layer
Characters execute jobs. Company systems operate continuously. Events emerge from thresholds and weighted rules. The player can click staff and departments to trace cause/effect.

## Satire rules
- Punch at incentives, contradictions, corporate language, hype, bureaucracy and human behavior.
- Never allege misconduct by a real person/company.
- No near-copy logos or slogans.
- Fictional competitors have mixed strengths/weaknesses. None maps one-to-one to a real company.

## MVP vertical slice
The first fun slice contains:
- One office floor.
- 6 staff roles.
- Build desks/server racks/safety lab.
- One research tree branch.
- Train/evaluate/release 3 model generations.
- 20 incidents.
- Pricing + users + compute cost.
- Trust/safety/cash interplay.
- One rival company.
- One regulator track.
- Save/load.
- 30–45 minute mini-campaign ending.
